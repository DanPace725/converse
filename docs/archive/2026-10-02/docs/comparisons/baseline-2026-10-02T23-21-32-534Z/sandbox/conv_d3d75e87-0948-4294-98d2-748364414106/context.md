# Recommended strategy

Make homelessness **rare, brief, and nonrecurring** by combining prevention, permanent housing, adequate care, safe interim accommodation, and housing-supply reform. Shelters are essential emergency infrastructure, but they cannot substitute for permanent housing.

## 1. Prevent housing loss

- Provide rapid assistance for rent arrears, utilities, deposits, and imminent eviction.
- Expand tenant legal representation and address unlawful screening, retaliation, and unsafe housing.
- Offer longer-term subsidies when a household faces a lasting affordability gap; one-time aid cannot solve that problem.
- Require coordinated housing planning before discharge from hospitals, behavioral-health facilities, corrections, and foster care.
- Connect residents to benefits, child care, transportation, and employment support without making employment a housing prerequisite.

**Accountability:** Measure actual housing stability—not simply the number of grants issued. Assistance received does not automatically mean homelessness was prevented.

## 2. Deliver permanent housing matched to need

Use several pathways rather than one universal program:

| Household need | Main response |
|---|---|
| Temporary financial disruption | Rapid rehousing and flexible assistance |
| Persistent rent–income gap | Long-term rental subsidy |
| Substantial, enduring support needs | Permanent supportive housing |
| Suitable existing housing opportunity | Affordable placement or verified, voluntary reunification |

Housing First should remove sobriety and treatment prerequisites while preserving ordinary tenancy obligations and actively offering care.

Create a landlord liaison team, reliable subsidy payments, accessible applications, reasonable accommodations, transparent prioritization, and an appeal process.

## 3. Expand affordable housing

- Permit more housing in suitable locations and remove unnecessary parking and permitting barriers.
- Use public land for housing with durable affordability requirements.
- Acquire and preserve threatened affordable buildings.
- Fund deeply affordable units—not only moderate-income housing.
- Assess conversions and inclusionary requirements for actual feasibility.
- Coordinate housing development with infrastructure and transit.

Track **announced, financed, completed, and occupied units separately**. Acquiring or leasing existing housing is not necessarily a net addition to regional supply.

## 4. Provide safe interim options and humane street response

Maintain accessible, low-barrier shelter and bridge accommodation, with appropriate options for families, couples, pets, disabilities, and trauma-related needs.

Outreach should combine housing navigation, peer support, sanitation, document recovery, and clinical assistance.

For planned encampment closures:
- Identify suitable destinations first.
- Provide legally required notice, transport, property protection, and storage.
- Account for disability, safety, family connections, and other practical barriers.
- Track residents’ destinations and subsequent housing outcomes.

**A cleared encampment is not a successful housing outcome.** Serious violence and immediate hazards still require appropriate public-safety responses.

## 5. Integrate health care and tailored support

Provide access to primary care, mental-health services, addiction treatment, harm reduction, medical respite, and tenancy support.

Develop distinct pathways for families, youth, survivors of violence, veterans, older adults, disabled residents, and people leaving incarceration. Housing allocation should not become a substitute for lawful clinical decision-making or due process.

## 6. Establish regional accountability

Create one accountable delivery office coordinating housing, health, benefits, outreach, institutions, and municipal partners.

Include:
- Paid lived-experience representation.
- Multi-year provider contracts and realistic staffing.
- Independent complaints and appeals.
- Quarterly public reporting.
- Independent evaluation and financial audit.
- Privacy protections and survivor confidentiality.

Do not count displacement into another municipality, incarceration, or unknown destinations as progress.
