## 2. Refinements to the previous analysis

### Scenario ranges are not promises

The Anthropic addendum's prevention-underperformance scenario gives 2,125 people at year five, compared with 2,000 initially. That is growth of 125, not a stock reduction. I therefore do not adopt its wording about committing to a reduction as large as that case. Publish adverse scenarios as risks, not successful target bounds. A scenario range is not a statistical confidence interval.

Retain three separate public statements: the aspirational outcome, the funded delivery schedule, and the downside scenarios with corrective actions. Do not lower an outcome target simply to declare success; explain any revision and its causes.

### Prevention yield needs a causal and unit-consistent definition

For evaluation, define causal yield as the difference in homelessness-entry risk between assistance and a credible comparison, over a specified follow-up period and among comparable households. The fraction who would become homeless without assistance is not by itself the fraction whose entry assistance prevents.

The base arithmetic treats 1,500 assistance episodes, 1,500 hypothetical entries, and person-based flow counts as compatible. Actual assistance can involve repeat transactions and multi-person households. Estimate household-level effects and convert to person-episode effects using observed composition and repeat-entry patterns. The model-specific 16.7% break-even threshold is not a universal program eligibility rule or a proven prevention success rate.

### Do not infer cost-effectiveness from mismatched prices

The addendum's roughly $13,333 per averted entry at a 30% yield is assistance spending divided by assumed averted entries. It is not an all-in or incremental cost-effectiveness estimate. Legal services, administration, repeat aid, uncertainty, household size, and other benefits matter. Comparing it with $26,000 for one year of supportive housing compares different outcomes, durations, and populations.

I retain prevention for its housing-stability and harm-reduction purposes, subject to evaluation and redesign; I do not adopt a rule that prevention is worthwhile only if this simple ratio beats supportive housing. Rapid-rehousing assistance can also be extended or repeated; it is not necessarily a single lifetime cost.

### Cost exclusions and launch costs require verification

The base plan requires confirmation of land within project costs; land is not established as wholly excluded from every project. Use a line-item audit rather than automatically adding land again. Intensive clinical capacity, respite, financing, inflation, and post-year-five commitments remain insufficiently costed.

Predevelopment, site inventories, legal review, data setup, recruitment, and staff training are not literally non-spending work. Service funding must support recruitment before occupancy, not arrive only once residents move in. Pay against an approved mobilization plan and actual ongoing capacity.

### Clinical readiness is a provider responsibility, not a treatment prerequisite

Require a credible support and escalation plan for the needs a project promises to serve. Do not make a person's housing contingent on accepting treatment or waiting for an ideal clinical system. If a project cannot safely deliver required support, fund an interim arrangement, partner support, or another suitable option through individualized review. Do not impose a blanket high-need exclusion.

## 3. Launch authorization and budget gates

Do not label the $560.875m example an adopted appropriation or an all-in cost. Keep its original scoped baseline and sensitivity tables visible, with an omissions ledger. Retain the 15% capital contingency assumption for modeling only; ring-fence any actual contingency and document authorized draws.

### Gate A: operating launch

Require an accountable sponsor; identified lawful funding for launch; access and urgent-response procedures; a privacy lead; provider staffing and continuity plans; and an initial expenditure ledger. Emergency help does not wait for the entire regional strategy to be approved.

Permit launch funding for prevention, legal assistance, outreach, landlord engagement, essential shelter corrections, benefits navigation, and delivery setup. Record existing versus incremental costs so the proposal does not accidentally count existing services twice. No new aggregate startup amount is defensible without knowing what already exists.

### Gate B: each placement tranche

Before committing additional recurring placements, verify: suitable housing availability; assistance duration and renewal plan; realistic rent and operating costs; inspection/accessibility requirements; staffing and support arrangements; payment authority; and grievance routes. A tranche is a procurement unit, not a limit on an individual's entitlement under applicable law.

Identify a named payer for rent, tenancy support, clinical services, and urgent after-hours response. An unsigned health-partner expectation is recorded as a gap, not committed funding.

### Gate C: capital project

Require site control or a viable acquisition agreement; independent condition and environmental assessment where relevant; accessibility review; rehabilitation/construction scope; all-in project budget and confirmed financing; permitting schedule; affordability terms; displacement prevention; and an operating plan.

Fund qualified predevelopment in parallel, with capped, approved exposure and stage-by-stage review. If delays undermine the occupancy schedule, compare leasing, acquisition, and new construction on suitability, life-cycle costs, and additional supply. Do not automatically substitute the cheapest unsuitable units.

### Gate D: annual continuation

At least annually, review the spending ledger, outcomes, workforce, resident feedback, supply schedule, and recurring funding risks. Protect existing tenants from program funding cliffs. Any provider change needs tenant continuity, records protection, and a funded transition.

For each funding line record: source; eligible uses; award status; start/end dates; amount; match; renewal risk; obligation; cash spending; responsible payer; and fallback. Separate capital grants, subsidies, clinical reimbursement, and unrestricted funds. The original modeled year-five $55m annual operating baseline excludes some needs and is not a complete sustainability estimate.

## 4. Practical service workflow and provisional standards

These time standards are my proposed launch service levels, not established legal deadlines or verified feasible staffing promises. Finalize them with providers, residents, and counsel; report performance and unmet need rather than denying help when a standard cannot be met.

| Stage | Proposed operating standard | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Telephone, in-person, and accessible digital routes; interpretation; no digital-only intake | Access lead |
| Immediate danger | Same-day safety triage and appropriate urgent referral; emergency response when needed | Duty team |
| Imminent eviction/discharge | Same-day screening for deadlines and urgent protection; use flexible funds as authority permits | Prevention/institutional lead |
| Routine request | Contact attempt within two business days; offer alternatives when unreachable | Intake provider |
| Coordination | Assign a named navigator within five business days of engagement when ongoing support is needed | Provider manager |
| Housing plan | Record household preferences, affordability gap, support needs, and next action; avoid repeated full assessments | Navigator |
| Lease-ready unit | Target inspection scheduling within five business days; never waive essential safety | Housing authority/inspections |
| Move-in | Confirm keys, usable utilities, accessibility, essential furnishings, subsidy payments, and support contacts | Placement provider |
| Follow-up | Offer contact within 72 hours of move-in and at approximately 30/90 days, tailored to need and choice | Tenancy team |
| Complaint | Acknowledge within two business days; urgent safety complaints triaged immediately | Independent complaints lead |

Do not require unnecessary identification documents, broad data consent, sobriety, or treatment attendance as generic intake prerequisites. Funding-specific legal eligibility still applies; arrange document recovery and alternative lawful routes. A declined unsuitable offer does not erase queue position or constitute a permanent refusal of help.

### Prevent predictable subsidy cliffs

Before time-limited assistance ends, reassess affordability and likely stability. Begin that review at least 60 days before a known termination where feasible. Options include extension, gradual taper, benefits corrections, voluntary transfer, long-term subsidy referral, or a resident-chosen sustainable alternative. Do not promise extensions without funding, but record unmet need and escalate it rather than merely close the case.

Use voluntary tenancy support and early arrears response. Tenants retain privacy and ordinary rights; a navigator is not a mandatory monitor. Follow-up loss is reported as missing information, not automatically as retention or failure.

## 5. Monthly flow model specification

This is a text specification and worked arithmetic, not a software implementation or fitted forecast.

### Accounting boundary

Choose a fixed geography and homelessness definition. Count people experiencing homelessness at month end separately from unique people experiencing it during a year. Distinguish first entries, returns, and movements across the boundary. Keep doubled-up and other housing-instability measures visible in separate compatible series.

For each month t:

**H(t+1) = H(t) + F(t) + R(t) + M_in(t) - P(t) - O(t) - M_out(t) + A(t)**

- H: observed or estimated homeless stock.
- F: first/other non-return homelessness-entry episodes inside the boundary.
- R: reentry episodes following a housing exit.
- M_in and M_out: cross-boundary changes not already counted in F, R, P, or O.
- P: verified permanent-housing exits from the homeless stock.
- O: separately classified non-housing exits, including deaths and institutionalization where they leave the defined stock.
- A: signed reconciliation adjustment for late reports, duplicates, revised estimates, or other explained corrections.

Never classify O, M_out, or a downward A as housing success. Leaving a service provider without leaving homelessness does not reduce H. An unknown destination is not presumed to be a verified exit from the regional stock. Transfers between shelter and street change accommodation, not overall homelessness.

Prevention effects enter a counterfactual projection, not as phantom exits from observed H. For a projection, estimate F_under_program = F_without_added_program - averted_entries, bounded by credible eligible risk and timing. Do not subtract prevention again if observed entry data already reflects it. The simplified original 1,500 annual entries includes returns when applicable; do not add R to that total without separating its components first.

Estimate returns by placement cohort and months since exit, rather than applying a percentage to all new exits immediately. Track censoring, follow-up completeness, and lawful regional linkage. Offer safe confidentiality-preserving approaches for survivors.

### Capacity constraints

The proposed P(t) cannot exceed the people actually ready and willing to take suitable housing, compatible units available, lease-up processing capacity, affordable subsidy commitments, and support delivery capacity. These constraints vary by household and housing type; a vacant studio is not capacity for a large family.

Track units, occupancy, resident count, turnovers, and subsidy duration separately. PSH admissions into new capacity are not the same as the number of additional occupied slots. Do not double-count a household when it changes programs while remaining housed. A backlog of assessments is not equivalent to a pool of suitable lease-ready matches.

### Worked ramp-up consequence

Use the base example's 2,000 starting stock, 1,500 entries, and 450 averted entries. If annualized exit capacity is 1,000 during the first half of year one and 1,250 during the second half, year-one exits total 1,125. If all later years achieve 1,250:

**2,000 + [1,500 - 450 - (0.5 × 1,000 + 0.5 × 1,250)] + 4 × (1,500 - 450 - 1,250) = 1,125 at year five.**

This calculated scenario is a 43.75% reduction, not 50%. It assumes prevention is already at full modeled effectiveness and omits other changes. Delayed prevention would worsen it. A budget showing staggered lease-up must also show the associated placement timing; apparent savings are not automatically efficiency gains.

If prevention averts only 225 annual entries, sustaining a flat stock requires 1,275 annual exits, and reaching 1,000 after five years requires **1,475**. The latter was independently recalculated from 1,500 - 225 + (2,000 - 1,000)/5. These requirements do not establish that extra exits can be purchased at the average price.

### Shelter check with downtime

With 300 beds, a 90-day average occupied stay, two average turnover days, and 90% utilization of the theoretical bed-cycle capacity, a illustrative throughput approximation is:

**300 × 365 × 0.90 / (90 + 2) = approximately 1,071 stay episodes per year.**

Here the 90% factor represents utilization after the explicit turnover assumption, not another measured occupied-bed rate; choose definitions carefully to avoid counting downtime twice. Stays are not unique people or permanent placements. Actual demand, household compatibility, accessibility, seasonal peaks, and length-of-stay distribution determine adequacy. Do not use this approximation to justify shelter closure or forced exits at day 90.

## 6. Weekly delivery management and escalation

Use a weekly operational meeting led by the delivery director with housing, prevention, shelter/outreach, clinical partners, and providers. Use minimal identifiable data only in authorized case coordination; public reporting is aggregated.

Review five queues: imminent housing losses; people without a safe interim option; suitable housing matches stalled before lease-up; current tenants at risk of losing housing; and units/projects blocked before availability. For each bottleneck record the owner, next action, deadline, and required resources. A meeting is not success unless the queue advances.

My proposed triggers:
- Any immediate safety failure or unlawful exclusion: same-day protective response and independent review as appropriate.
- Rent-payment disruption threatening an existing tenancy: urgent finance intervention; do not wait for a quarterly dashboard.
- Two consecutive months below 90% of the approved, ramp-adjusted placement schedule: a 30-day bottleneck correction plan, allowing for seasonality and data revisions.
- A project slipping a critical milestone: update the occupancy forecast immediately and present a substitution/mitigation option at the next monthly review.
- Missing or incompatible denominators: suspend causal or comparative claims, not essential services; repair the measurement.

These are administrative review thresholds, not penalties, automatic budget cuts, or tenant sanctions. Review complex-case mix and adverse selection before reallocating contracts. Maintain the base plan's quarterly public accountability alongside monthly management.

## 7. Siting, public trust, and resident participation

Start engagement before sites are finalized when practical, without creating a neighborhood veto over lawful housing or revealing future residents' identities. Publish site-selection criteria: accessibility, transit/services, suitability, cost, available infrastructure, opportunity, and avoidance of concentrated disadvantage.

Pay lived-experience advisors and prospective residents for participation. Offer language access, childcare/accessibility support, and confidential feedback. Explain operational safety, property maintenance, contact points, and complaint procedures. Separate evidence about site operations from unsupported generalizations about homeless residents.

Use one neighborhood contact and a resident-protective escalation route. Address maintenance, sanitation, noise, and credible safety complaints through ordinary fair standards. Do not promise zero incidents, mandatory surveillance, or expulsion for refusing services. Report actual operations and corrections without public resident-level data.

## 8. Evidence, procurement, and decisions still requiring facts

Before local adoption, produce an evidence-and-authority ledger with: claim; population/intervention; source and publication date; study design; relevant outcomes; transferability limits; current legal/funding review; reviewer; and verification status. Potential sources listed in the base plan are a research agenda, not citations already checked.

Priority verification tasks: achievable prevention effects; subsidy duration and returns; PSH housing stability and care intensity; eviction legal assistance; local rent/availability; project costs; staffing; and privacy/legal authority. Separate credible housing-stability evidence from more variable health, employment, and fiscal effects. Use fair ethical evaluation; no withholding urgent protection to manufacture a comparison group.

Procurement specifications should require resident rights, accessibility, safeguarding, staffing, after-hours escalation appropriate to program intensity, payment reliability, data minimization, outcome definitions, audited costs, and transition plans. Small trusted providers may need technical assistance and working capital to participate; complex procurement must not unnecessarily exclude them.

Unknowns that materially affect the plan remain: jurisdiction; actual geography/population definitions; sheltered/unsheltered and annual flows; current spending/capacity; rent and vacancy; clinical agreements; recurring funds; unit pipeline; and lawful eligibility. Do not invent them. The useful decision now is to make funding and forecasts conditional on their verification while proceeding with justified urgent service and planning work.
