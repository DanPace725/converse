## 4. Launch sequence and accountable owners

Dates below are relative to local authorization of the planning process, not a promised user-specified deadline. Owners are required roles, not identified agencies or people.

### Days 1–30: establish control and protect urgent needs

**Accountable lead:** establish a small delivery office with housing, finance, health, legal, procurement, accessibility and data responsibilities; appoint paid lived-experience advisers and a rights/complaints reviewer. Inventory current residents and essential services before changing contracts.

**Finance lead:** build a funding register recording source, permitted uses, amount, appropriation status, match, reimbursement lag, expiry, renewal risk and commitments. Separate gross program cost, existing funded activity, incremental funding need and cash requirements. Appropriated, applied-for and hoped-for funds must be distinct.

**Service lead:** maintain emergency weather/safety response and an accessible prevention route using existing lawful resources. Do not postpone lifesaving services until the dashboard is finished. Establish survivor-confidential referrals, family unity, interpreters, disability accommodations, safe belongings handling and non-digital access.

**Data/rights leads:** agree on consent, minimum necessary data, access controls, separate survivor systems where required, small-cell suppression, complaint/appeal handling and an incident escalation process. Optional sharing is not a condition of assistance.

### Days 31–60: convert the budget into deliverable capacity

**Housing lead:** assemble a verified inventory of occupied supported units, genuine vacancies, landlords, accessible units, rehabilitation candidates and staffing. No unverified vacant-unit count may be booked as available capacity.

**Procurement lead:** request costed proposals with full rent/support/delivery overhead, caseloads, staffing plan, referral rules, accessibility, household mix, grievance protections, start dates and recurring obligations. Independently price the health, legal and central-function lines; labels alone do not justify $16m of service/administrative spending.

**Institutional partners:** establish lawful hospital, corrections, foster-care and treatment discharge agreements with named navigators, bridge options and continuity of care. Do not delay medically necessary care or lawful release because a housing placement is missing.

### Days 61–90: approve only a feasible first tranche

**Lead and finance:** publish the baseline, capacity/funding gaps and a 12-month cash-flow forecast. Submit actual appropriations and contracts for the appropriate approval. Release only supported commitments, rather than an arbitrary percentage of the illustrative budget.

**Housing and clinical leads:** implement distinct affordability-only, rapid-rehousing and supportive-housing routes with household choice, reassessment and appeal. Begin eligible ongoing assistance only after its funding gate passes. Start lawful leasing and prevention expansion where providers and units are ready.

**Capital lead:** submit site-level appraisals. No unconditional acquisition/construction commitment until title/control, approvals, building condition, environmental issues, accessibility, infrastructure, displacement protections, cost contingencies, procurement and operating support are resolved. Staged feasibility work may precede this gate if lawfully authorized.

### Months 4–12 and years 2–3

Publish monthly operational results and quarterly public reviews. Add service tranches as workforce, units and finance permit; do not compensate for missing housing by expanding indefinite shelter stays. Deliver appraised capital projects as ready, not to an invented quota. At least annually, refresh recurring obligations, household rent gaps, staffing and the capital pipeline. Commission an independent implementation/equity review in year two and an outcomes/cost review in year three. A funding gap approaching within 12 months requires escalation before further permanent commitments.

## 5. Household allocation and continuity rules

1. **Respond to immediate danger first:** accessible safety, survivor confidentiality, acute health response and weather protection. This does not require a clinical diagnosis to qualify for help.
2. **Assess the actual housing barrier:** imminent loss, rent-income gap, landlord/access discrimination, document problems, or ongoing tenancy/support needs. Assessments are aids, not opaque automatic denial scores.
3. **Choose a pathway with the household:** prevention when safe housing can be retained; rapid rehousing when limited support is reasonably likely to suffice; ongoing subsidy for a persistent affordability gap; supportive housing for substantial continuing support needs. Family size and disability accessibility affect placement, not entitlement to dignity.
4. **Triage limited capacity transparently:** urgency, vulnerability, time waiting, and suitability of the actual available unit, with human review and appeal. A hard-to-house applicant must not be refused merely to protect performance statistics. Public eligibility standards remain subject to local law and restricted funding.
5. **Prevent subsidy cliffs:** review housing stability and rent affordability before temporary assistance ends, leaving time to arrange a lawful extension or transition. Do not promise an ongoing slot that is not funded. Lack of a slot triggers escalation and bridge planning, not concealment of need.
6. **Verify housing quality and household suitability:** a nominal vacancy or shelter bed is insufficient if unsafe, inaccessible or incompatible with necessary family/survivor arrangements. Refusal of an unsuitable offer is not evidence of lack of housing need.

## 6. Measurement corrections and practical demand model

### Qualification of Appendix A

Anthropic / claude-sonnet-5-5's hypothetical steady-state example uses **mean** duration. The original Section 10 target uses **median** episode duration. A 30% fall in the median does not necessarily imply a 30% fall in the mean, especially with a long tail of persistent homelessness. Therefore the approximately 14.3% inflow-reduction result applies only if mean duration also falls by 30%, inflow and duration definitions align, and steady-state assumptions hold. It does not directly validate the original median target or predict a three-year transition.

Also qualify Appendix A's description of $450m as a ceiling: it is the original scenario total under fixed assumptions, not a user-approved cap. Lower first-year utilization might reduce cash outlays, while inflation, higher costs or additional obligations might increase them.

### Operational accounting

Choose one consistent population and geography for each series, reporting people and households separately. For monthly stock, use:

**Ending homeless population = beginning population + first entries + returns + net transfers into scope − verified permanent-housing exits − other exits from scope.**

Shelter/street transfers within scope do not change total homelessness. Deaths, institutional stays, geographic departures and lost contact must not be reported as permanent-housing successes. Investigate unknown destinations; do not assume they are successful exits. Record classification corrections separately and reconcile stocks with observed flows. Return episodes must not disappear from the entry measure.

For the simple steady-state relationship, use total episode-entry rate (including return episodes) and the arithmetic mean duration of corresponding episodes. Person counts, household counts and episode counts are not interchangeable. Unique annual entrants alone are insufficient where repeat episodes are common. Track ongoing/censored episodes rather than computing duration only from people who already exited; use appropriate time-to-event methods for final evaluation.

### Minimum dashboard

Publish: first entries and returns; verified permanent exits; mean and median episode duration with methods/missingness; 6/12/24-month retention and returns; waits; accessible housing availability; prevention access and subsequent stability; provider vacancies; spending/commitments/cash; safety incidents; and complaints/appeals. Break down access and outcomes appropriately while protecting confidential and small-cell data.

For retention, define the cohort, denominator, observation period, moves between permanent homes, deaths, missing follow-up and funding termination before computing rates. Do not drop unfavorable or unknown cases from the denominator without reporting them. Prevention recipients who remain housed are not automatically proven causal prevention successes; use ethical evaluation and avoid withholding essential aid for research.

These are administrative planning defaults, not externally validated clinical or legal standards.

| Trigger | Required action |
|---|---|
| Immediate serious safety incident, discriminatory denial or privacy breach | Escalate promptly to the relevant safety/rights authority; safeguard people, contain affected data flows, investigate and preserve access through safe alternatives |
| Three consecutive months of net population growth, rising placement waits or worsening return trends | Lead publishes a diagnostic/corrective plan within 30 days; check count quality, inflow, landlord access, rent gaps, discharge failures and service capacity before choosing reallocations |
| Material disparity in denial, waits or stability after accounting for data limitations | Independent review of criteria and practice; engage affected groups and change barriers rather than assuming applicant deficits |
