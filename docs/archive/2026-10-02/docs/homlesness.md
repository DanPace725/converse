
Build the next layer of the plan by focusing on the parts that are still structurally underdeveloped. In particular:

- Design the actual operating/governance architecture: who controls funding, who owns outcomes, how regional agencies/providers coordinate, what authority sits at each level, and how disputes or failures are handled.
- Develop an intake, triage, and service-allocation system that avoids creating a giant bureaucratic bottleneck. Explain how prevention, shelter/interim housing, rapid rehousing, permanent supportive housing, and long-term rental assistance connect as one system rather than separate programs.
- Work out what happens when demand exceeds capacity. Establish explicit prioritization, waitlist, appeal, reassessment, and emergency-override rules.
- Develop the provider contracting/procurement model: what gets paid for, what outcomes matter, what perverse incentives to avoid, how poor providers are corrected or replaced, and where outcome-based funding is useful versus dangerous.
- Build the landlord/housing-supply pipeline in operational terms. Include recruitment, risk mitigation, inspection, damage funds, vacancy coordination, master leasing where appropriate, and how the system prevents housing search from becoming the throughput bottleneck.
- Add a workforce architecture: roles, indicative caseload logic, supervision, burnout/turnover controls, lived-experience roles, and what to do when staffing rather than money is the limiting constraint.
- Develop the data and accountability layer: minimum common data model, privacy constraints, deduplication, longitudinal outcomes, cohort tracking, public reporting, auditing, and safeguards against optimizing easy metrics while ignoring difficult cases.
- Add failure-mode planning. Identify several ways the whole system could fail even while individual programs appear successful, and design early-warning indicators and corrective actions.
- Develop a surge protocol for recession, rent spikes, disasters, extreme weather, shelter loss, or sudden migration/displacement.
- Work out the political/community implementation problem around siting without turning the plan into a public-relations essay. Define decision rules, legitimate community input, boundaries on veto power, and mechanisms for addressing real neighborhood impacts.
- Address rural implementation separately where density, transportation, provider availability, and housing stock make the metro model inappropriate.
- Reconcile the existing documents into one coherent policy architecture. Identify genuine conflicts or obsolete assumptions and make a decision about which approach becomes the current working version rather than merely listing the disagreement.
